# Reusable Prompt: Healthy 7-Day Vegetarian South Indian Meal Plan

Copy this prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or another AI chat tool. Edit only the **FOR block** at the top when you want a different plan.

## EDITABLE FOR BLOCK

- **Age:** [enter age]
- **Activity level:** [low / light / moderate / high; briefly describe normal activity]
- **Goal:** Work towards gradual, sustainable weight loss
- **Location / food availability:** [town or district, Tamil Nadu / South India]
- **People eating:** [number of people; state whether portions are for one adult or need scaling]
- **Weekday cooking time:** [for example, 20-30 minutes]
- **Season / current local produce:** [summer / monsoon / winter; optional local vegetables and fruits]
- **Allergies / foods to avoid for safety:** [write "none" or list clearly]
- **Dislikes:** [list foods or write "none"]
- **Other preferences:** [spice level, budget, fasting/cultural preferences, kitchen equipment]
- **Health considerations:** [optional; do not include private details unless you choose to]

## PROMPT

### ROLE
Act as a nutrition education assistant who understands everyday South Indian vegetarian cooking. Create a practical meal-plan draft using familiar ingredients and balanced meals. Do not claim to be a doctor or dietitian.

### TASK
Create a healthy **7-day vegetarian South Indian meal plan** for the person described in the editable FOR block. Cover all seven days, with exactly four eating occasions each day:
1. Breakfast
2. Lunch
3. Snack
4. Dinner

### CONTEXT
The person is working towards weight loss and needs a realistic plan that can fit normal home cooking. Use recognisable South Indian dishes and ingredients that are commonly available in the stated location. Prefer seasonal local vegetables and fruits when suitable. Plan portions for the stated number of people, and clearly label whether a portion is per person or for the whole household.

### CONSTRAINTS AND GUARDRAILS
- Keep the plan vegetarian (no meat, fish or eggs by default; include eggs only if the FOR block explicitly says ovo-vegetarian). Include a sensible variety of cereals/millets, pulses or legumes, vegetables, fruit and suitable dairy or other vegetarian protein sources where appropriate.
- Use real dish names, for example idli with sambar, dosa, adai, ven pongal, upma, pesarattu, keerai kootu, poriyal, sundal, rasam, curd rice and vegetable sambar. Choose dishes that fit the person's preferences and location.
- Use ordinary household portions such as cups, idlis, dosas, bowls, ladles or pieces. Make portions practical, not extreme or unusually small.
- Aim for balanced meals and variety across the week. Avoid repeating the same main dish every day.
- Respect every allergy and food to avoid. Mention cross-contact precautions when an allergy is listed.
- Respect dislikes. Do not hide disliked ingredients inside chutneys, podis, gravies or mixed dishes.
- Prefer ingredients available in local markets. Do not build the plan around quinoa, avocado, imported berries, expensive diet products or unfamiliar ingredients unless the FOR block specifically asks for them.
- Keep weekday recipes simple and compatible with the stated cooking time. Suggest sensible batch preparation only when useful.
- Do not prescribe fasting, meal skipping, detoxes, supplements, crash diets, very-low-calorie diets or rapid weight-loss targets.
- Do not give a calorie target or promise a specific amount of weight loss. Do not label foods as "good" or "bad".
- If the FOR block mentions pregnancy, breastfeeding, a medical condition, a history of disordered eating, or a medically significant allergy, explain that the plan needs review by a qualified dietitian or clinician. Do not provide disease-specific dietary treatment.
- If essential information is missing, state reasonable assumptions briefly rather than inventing personal facts.

### RESPONSE FORMAT
Return the main plan as **one day-by-day Markdown table** with exactly these columns:

| Day | Meal | Dish | Portion |

Include exactly 28 meal rows: Monday to Sunday, with Breakfast, Lunch, Snack and Dinner for each day in that order. State portions in ordinary household measures and make clear whether they are per person. Do not omit a meal.

After the table, add only these short sections:
1. **Preparation notes:** up to five practical tips to reduce weekday cooking time.
2. **Balance check:** one short paragraph explaining how the week includes variety across grains/millets, pulses/legumes, vegetables, fruit and vegetarian protein.
3. **Assumptions and safety note:** list any assumptions and remind the reader that this is a general educational draft, not personalised dietary advice. A qualified dietitian should review a plan for individual medical needs.

Keep the format and column names the same every time this prompt is used.
